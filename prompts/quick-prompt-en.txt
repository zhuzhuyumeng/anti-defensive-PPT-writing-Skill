Apply these principles to my presentation content:

Identify the audience, communication task, duration or slide count, and required content. Do not ask again for supplied information. State reasonable preference assumptions and proceed. Note missing facts once in the draft; do not put missing-evidence markers on the slide, and do not invent numbers or cases.

Build a narrative around one supported core message rather than allocating equal space to every activity. Choose a decision, research, retrospective, product, or teaching path that fits the task. Preserve specified templates and sections.

Give each slide one main audience question. Use informative titles within the evidence; label unvalidated content as a question or recommendation. Write concrete, usable on-slide copy that keeps only the judgment and key numbers. Put optional explanation in speaker cues and full detail in appendices.

Give charts an evidentiary job and a clear focus. Keep the units a number needs to be readable. Do not invent chart data, and do not present a conceptual illustration as a measured result. Do not put qualifications, limitations, sources, risks, scope-of-use notes, or missing-evidence markers in titles, on-slide copy, or chart labels.

Remove empty apologies and self-disparagement while retaining real losses and negative results when they are the point of the slide. Do not generalize a local pilot, turn correlation into causation, or invent a benefit to explain a weakness. Narrow claims when evidence is insufficient; do not invent or exaggerate.

Order slides by understanding or decision needs, merge repetition, remove irrelevant content, and adapt density to a talk or a reading deck. Keep on-slide copy brief: do not restate a title that already makes the point, state each fact once, prefer short phrases, numbers, and contrasts, and move reasoning into speaker cues. Do not fill a slide with information already given. A closing slide should keep the conclusion, decision, or request, not relist earlier titles. End by completing the audience's task with a finding, application, or concrete request.

By default, deliver the core message, narrative order, and each slide's title and on-slide copy. Do not add audience-task, display-suggestion, or evidence-and-scope fields on every slide, and do not add a default off-slide caveats block. Keep qualifications, limitations, sources, risks, scope-of-use notes, and missing-evidence markers out of the on-slide copy, and do not move them into a default speaker cue. Add a speaker cue only when a talk needs it. Match smaller requests such as a one-slide edit or an outline. If I request a PPTX or HTML artifact, complete it with available authoring tools.
