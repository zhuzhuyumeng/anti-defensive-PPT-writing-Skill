Apply these principles to my presentation content:

Identify the audience, communication task, duration or slide count, and required content. Do not ask again for supplied information. State reasonable preference assumptions and proceed; mark missing facts without inventing numbers or cases.

Build a narrative around one supported core message rather than allocating equal space to every activity. Choose a decision, research, retrospective, product, or teaching path that fits the task. Preserve specified templates and sections.

Give each slide one main audience question. Use informative titles within the evidence; label unvalidated content as a question or recommendation. Write concrete, usable on-slide copy. Put optional explanation in speaker cues and full detail in appendices.

Give charts an evidentiary job and a clear focus. Retain units, dates, sample sizes, comparison conditions, and sources needed to interpret them. Do not invent chart data; label illustrations and examples. Keep material risks and scope visible on the relevant slide, not only in notes.

Remove empty apologies and self-disparagement while retaining real losses, negative results, and material uncertainty. Do not generalize a local pilot, turn correlation into causation, or invent a benefit to explain a weakness. Narrow claims when evidence is insufficient.

Order slides by understanding or decision needs, merge repetition, remove irrelevant content, and adapt density to a talk or a reading deck. Keep on-slide copy brief: do not restate a title that already makes the point, state each fact once, prefer short phrases, numbers, and contrasts, and move reasoning into speaker cues. Do not fill a slide with information already given. A closing slide should keep the decision, scope, or request, not relist earlier titles. End by completing the audience's task with a finding, application, or concrete request.

By default, deliver the core message, narrative order, and each slide's title and on-slide copy. Do not add audience-task, display-suggestion, or evidence-and-scope fields on every slide; put needed qualifications in the on-slide copy. Add a speaker cue only when a talk needs it. Match smaller requests such as a one-slide edit or an outline. If I request a PPTX or HTML artifact, complete it with available authoring tools.
